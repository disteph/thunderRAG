--- RESPONSE ---
IMAP (Internet Message Access Protocol) and POP3 (Post Office Protocol version 3) are two different methods for retrieving emails from a server to your local device. Here’s how they differ:

**IMAP:**
- Allows access to your email account without downloading the messages to your computer or device.
- Emails remain stored on the server, allowing you to manage and read them across multiple devices while maintaining synchronization between them.
- Useful for collaboration and shared accounts where several people may need to check the same mailbox simultaneously.
- Supports features like folders, flags (marking emails as read/unread), and message tagging.

**POP3:**
- Downloads email messages from a server to your device or computer.
- Once downloaded, messages are typically deleted from the server or left there based on configuration settings.
- Does not provide synchronization across devices; each device has its own set of emails unless configured otherwise.
- Simpler setup and less resource-intensive since it stores minimal data locally.

Choose IMAP if you need access to your email through multiple devices at once, while POP3 is better suited for accessing your email from one primary device.
